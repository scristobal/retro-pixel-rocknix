011 - ROCKER MYSTERY (UNSOLVED)
==============================================================

Problem statement
-----------------
The RetroPixel Pocket has two physical "rockers" (small flat tabs you
can tilt up, tilt down, or push straight in):
    - Volume rocker
    - Brightness rocker

Each is mechanically a 3-action switch (up tilt, down tilt, centre
press), so 6 actions total across both rockers.

We can't get all 6 actions to fire predictable, consistent keycodes.

What we know about the candidate GPIOs
--------------------------------------
The factory BSP joypad node lists six GPIOs that are "rocker-like" —
i.e. not the D-pad, ABXY, L, R, SELECT, or START:

    gpio2 PA0     BSP label "BACKUP"        BSP linux,code BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY5
    gpio2 PA1     BSP label "BACKDOWN"      BSP linux,code BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY6
    gpio2 PA2     BSP label "F3"            BSP linux,code BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY3
    gpio2 PA4     BSP label "F4"            BSP linux,code BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY4
    gpio3 PB2     BSP label "TOP-LEFT2"     BSP linux,code BTN_TL2
    gpio3 PB7     BSP label "TOP-RIGHT2"    BSP linux,code BTN_TR2

The BSP labels are misleading — they were inherited from the RG351MP /
ODROID-GO Super reference design which had L2/R2 and brightness keys
in a different layout.  On RPPocket those names DO NOT correspond to
the physical actions.

Test 1 — captures during boot (commits 4d983b6f34 + 3d6450b517)
----------------------------------------------------------------
We ran 'evtest --grab' for 60s (later 120s) on every /dev/input/event*
during boot, asked the user to press each rocker action in a
documented order, and copied the logs out via post.sh.

Two consecutive captures both produced this pattern (filtered to
press-down events only):

    User intent                    Fired GPIO    Fired keycode
    ---------------------------    ----------    -------------
    "Volume tilt UP"               gpio3 PB7     BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY4
    "Volume tilt DOWN"             gpio2 PA0     KEY_BRIGHTNESSUP
    "Volume centre press"          gpio2 PA1     KEY_BRIGHTNESSDOWN
    "Brightness tilt UP"           gpio3 PB2     BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY3
    "Brightness tilt DOWN"         (no event fired)
    "Brightness centre press"      (no event fired)

So 4 out of 6 actions fire some GPIO; the remaining 2 brightness-side
actions don't fire any of the 18 button GPIOs we have wired up.
PA2 / PA4 in particular never fire across two test runs.

Based on those captures we shipped a "best guess" mapping (commit 3d6450b517):

    gpio3 PB7  -> KEY_VOLUMEUP                (vol tilt UP)
    gpio2 PA0  -> KEY_VOLUMEDOWN              (vol tilt DOWN)
    gpio2 PA1  -> BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY1          (vol centre)
    gpio3 PB2  -> KEY_BRIGHTNESSUP            (brt tilt UP)
    gpio2 PA2  -> KEY_BRIGHTNESSDOWN          (brt tilt DOWN, GUESS)
    gpio2 PA4  -> BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY2          (brt centre, GUESS)

Test 2 — real-use feedback after that mapping shipped
-----------------------------------------------------
With the above DTS, the user reported on actual usage:

    "Volume centre press"          increases system volume
                                   (i.e. some GPIO is firing KEY_VOLUMEUP,
                                   so PB7 fires when they press the
                                   volume rocker centre)

    "Brightness up tilt"           decreases system volume
                                   (i.e. some GPIO is firing KEY_VOLUMEDOWN,
                                   so PA0 fires when they press the
                                   brightness rocker UP)

This contradicts test 1.  Either:

    (A) The physical actions the user labelled in test 1 don't
        correspond to what they label the same way today.  The
        rockers are small and ambiguous to feel: pushing "up" on a
        rocker often partially engages a centre switch; pushing
        "centre" partially engages an edge switch.
    (B) The rockers have only 1 or 2 distinct switches each, not 3.
        What we believed were three separate physical actions might
        be just one (or two) reachable by different finger positions.
    (C) The rockers are on different GPIOs than the BSP suggests
        (i.e. PA2/PA4 are not wired at all on RPPocket; the brightness
        rocker uses GPIOs we haven't enumerated).

We DO NOT know which of A, B, C is correct.

Open follow-up
--------------
The user needs to do a CLEAR-LABEL re-test that pins the physical
action to the GPIO unambiguously:

    1. Push only on the UPPER edge of the volume rocker.
       What does the system do?
    2. Push only on the LOWER edge of the volume rocker.
       What does the system do?
    3. Push STRAIGHT IN on the middle of the volume rocker.
       What does the system do?
    4-6. Same three actions for the brightness rocker.

With the current DTS mapping, the visible reaction tells us the GPIO:

    "system volume goes UP"        the GPIO is gpio3 PB7   (KEY_VOLUMEUP)
    "system volume goes DOWN"      the GPIO is gpio2 PA0   (KEY_VOLUMEDOWN)
    "screen brightness changes"    the GPIO is gpio3 PB2 or gpio2 PA2
                                   (KEY_BRIGHTNESSUP / DOWN; userspace
                                   may not bind these to anything visible)
    "nothing happens"              either gpio2 PA1 / PA4 (TRIGGER_HAPPY,
                                   no userspace handler), OR no GPIO
                                   fired at all (the action is not
                                   wired to anything we listen on)

Once we have those 6 visible reactions reliably reported, finalising
the rocker mapping is a 5-minute DTS edit.

Things we already tried that ruled out classes of bugs
-------------------------------------------------------
    - Pinctrl is correctly muxing all 6 candidate pins to GPIO with
      pull-up.  Verified at runtime via /sys/kernel/debug/gpio output
      (all 6 lines listed as 'in hi IRQ ACTIVE LOW').
    - All 18 GPIOs request IRQs; gpio-keys is the registered device.
    - The autostart hook's evtest --grab was confirmed as a
      DIFFERENT bug (it blocked ROCKNIX from reading buttons during
      the first ~2.5 minutes of boot).  Removed in commit b03b770f51.
    - We are NOT looking at the amux ADC.  The factory joypad driver
      uses amux only for analog-stick X/Y axes; rocker BUTTONS are
      plain GPIOs.

What is definitely not the answer
---------------------------------
    - "Just use the odroidgo3-joypad driver."  That driver reads the
      same 6 GPIOs the same way; it would not produce different
      events.

Where to look if you pick this up
---------------------------------

                                     press-down 'value 1' events)
    debug/post.out                   full post.sh dump from the last
                                     run, including pinmux and gpio

                                     from images/extracted/v1.0.img
                                     if missing)

Honest assessment
-----------------
This is the only piece blocking the port.  If hypothesis B is right
(rockers have fewer switches than expected), we can ship as-is with
just 4 keycodes assigned and call the others 'reserved'.
